Redo it: much quieter, softer and duller (a scuff on stone, a little different each time), fewer of them when walking freely, and maybe a recorded set of footsteps (stone, wet stone, wading, cobbles in the rain) like the monsters have now.

## Bigger ideas

- [x] **Start every new game with the same two pre-made wretches in the Gaol.** Done: Varg Hollowtooth (Fanged Deserter) and Wren Sixpence (Gutterborn Scum), `PAIR` / `rollPair()` in `js/rules.js`; a new game starts with them, and *Roll your own instead* is a smaller button under them. Not done: recording the Gaol's lines again with their names (the narrator still leaves names out). It's a roguelite and wretches die a lot, but the first run should get people out of the prison without much hassle, give them a feel for the game, and let them beat some people up. Two fixed characters would also be a better way into the story; once the larger story is under way, it matters less who carries it on. After them, new wretches are rolled or made from the book as now (the heirs who wake at a class home in Skarnvik, `startCreate` / `acceptParty` in `js/game.js`). Still to decide:
  - who they are: names, classes, a line of who they were and why they were in the Brine;
  - their abilities, HP and gear, tuned so the Gaol is winnable but still dangerous;
  - decent weapons waiting for them when they get into the storage area (the evidence room, where `restoreGear` gives your things back);
  - what the title screen offers: a new game always starts with the two, and rolling or making your own is for the heirs;
  - with fixed names, the narrator could say them; the lines that say "one of you" could be recorded again with their names for the Gaol.
- [ ] **A solid game loop, and telling the player about it up front.** Wretches are meant to die. You take each pair as far as you can, they die, new ones start, and what the dead found and did stays unlocked, so each run gets further. The player should know from the start that they won't keep these characters for the whole game. How it works now:
  - dying in the Gaol keeps the Gaol as the dead left it: two new rolled wretches wake in the sealed cell at the end of the cell row, and go to the evidence room for their own things and the long key, back on its hook (the Warden's hall locks again while he lives; `newPairInGaol` in `js/game.js`, `sealedcell` / `rewake` in `js/story.js`);
  - dying after the escape keeps the world: heirs, rolled up at a class home in Skarnvik, carry on what the dead learned (scenes seen, flags, keys, opened ways; `gameOver` / `acceptParty` in `js/game.js`);
  - the Codex (strange items you've worn or wielded) lasts across every run in this browser;
  - the Calendar of Nechrubel: at the seventh Misery the world ends and everything goes (`worldEnds`).

  Still to decide:
  - what a "run" is (one pair's lives?), and what exactly carries over: story progress, shortcuts (the Gaol already escaped, a way down already opened), the Codex, money or gear left at the dead pair's grave for the next ones to find, new classes unlocked by how far someone got;
  - whether dying in the Gaol should also carry something over, or stay a short tutorial you replay;
  - whether the Calendar running out is the real "you lost", and what's kept even then;
  - how the player is told before they start: a short box or page at the first new game ("Your wretches will die. That's the game…"), and the death screen saying what was kept and what's next ("Kept: the gravedigger's key, 2 new Codex entries. Somebody else in Skarnvik heard about it.");
  - a list of the dead (a graveyard or roll on the title screen) so the runs add up to something you can see.
- [ ] **A journal.** One place to read what's going on and what you're meant to be doing, so you can put the game down and pick it up again. It goes with the game loop above.
  - **The story so far**, a line or two at a time, written as it happens: captured and thrown in the Brine Gaol, out of the cell, the ledger found, the Warden dead, up into Skarnvik…
  - **Open quests**, with what's known about each. In the Gaol: get out of the cell, find the Warden's key, get your things back from the evidence room, find the way out. Later, the open-world ones: the Stranger's errands, the bounty board, the kin and the freed man, Saint Agne. Done ones get ticked and stay readable.
  - **Kept per pair.** Each pair of wretches gets its own chapter: who they were, what they did, how and where they died. The chapters stay after they die, so the journal becomes the record of every run, along with what's been achieved across all of them.
  - **When a pair dies with quests open**, the quests aren't handed on automatically. The journal notes it ("Varg and Wren were looking for the bell-ringers' door when they died in the Undercroft"), and the next pair takes the quest up again by going back to the person or place it came from. The quest stays in the journal (greyed or marked) so you remember it's there.
  - Today there's only the one-line `goal()` in `js/game.js` (logged after the Stranger and on waking) and `S.flags` / `S.scenes`; quests would need their own list in the save (id, title, steps, which pair took them, and whether it's done, open or dropped by the dead), plus a button for the journal next to Map and Party (mind the mobile pass).
  - The narrator doesn't read the journal: it's for reading, with no recordings (so `Q.Voice` must not try to speak it).
  - Spell everything out: the journal says exactly what to do and where ("Find the long key: it hangs in the evidence room, through the infirmary"). Nobody should get lost.
- [ ] **Death has consequences, and the world moves on.** Dying shouldn't just swap in two new wretches; time passes and things get worse.
  - **The Gaol: two chances.** When the first pair dies, a day passes and the water rises (deeper everywhere, the lower block harder going); the death screen and the journal warn that if these two die too, the Gaol drowns. If the second pair dies, the Gaol floods completely: the run is over and a new game starts with Varg and Wren.
  - **After the escape: time passes before the next pair.** A death with no side quests open: the heirs pick it up a day later. A death with side quests open: a week later, and each open side quest has its own permanent outcome (someone dies, a place is lost, a price goes up, a reward is gone), written into the journal and shown in the town.
  - **The Calendar is the clock.** Every day lost brings the Miseries closer; the seventh still ends the world. The main quest never fails on its own; the Calendar is what runs out.
  - Needs the journal first (quests and their states), and new narration for each outcome.
